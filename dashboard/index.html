Water quality in the Gowanus Canal

Volunteer and public-programme monitoring of a Superfund waterway under active dredging — bacteria, dissolved oxygen and construction turbidity, drawn from one database.

Rain drives the bacteria, not the season

Each dot is one Enterococcus sample. Rainfall is the 72-hour total ending on the collection day, from the monitoring programme's own Central Park gauge figures. The count scale is logarithmic because bacteria counts span four orders of magnitude. Lab results are censored at both ends — readings reported as “<10” and “>24196” sit on the top and bottom gridlines rather than at a true value.

Share of samples unsafe to swim, by year

Percentage of samples above the 110 MPN threshold each season. Fourteen years in, there is no downward trend — the canal has not been getting cleaner on this measure.

Sample counts vary by year (19–60), so single-year moves are noisy.

Bacteria by sampling site

Median Enterococcus count. The upper canal is consistently worse: it is furthest from the harbour, so it flushes least.

Medians, not averages — a single storm sample can be 100× the typical value. Site sample counts range from 18 to 257.

Dissolved oxygen: how much of the time can the canal support fish?

Gowanus Dredgers sonde readings, restricted to located in-water samples. Bars show the mean; the marker shows the 10th percentile — the level on a bad day, which is what actually kills fish. Sites with fewer than 100 readings are omitted.

Construction turbidity through two dredging phases

Weekly median cloudiness at the ambient monitoring buoy during EPA's dredging programme. RTA1 and RTA2 are different stretches of canal, monitored in sequence by different buoys, and monitored differently: RTA1 ran around the clock, RTA2 only on weekdays, 07:00–17:00. Two series, not one continuous record.

Weekly medians. Short dredging plumes run far higher than this line suggests — RTA2's worst week hits 411 NTU at the 90th percentile against a median of 47 — so the 90th percentile sits in the tooltip and the data table rather than on the axis, where it would flatten RTA1's entire range into a straight line. The medians are still comparable across phases: applying RTA2's daytime-weekday window to RTA1, which has both, moves its median from 6.31 to 6.30 NTU. Event capture is not — a plume at night or at a weekend is invisible in RTA2. The first weeks of each phase read near zero, which is almost certainly instrument commissioning rather than exceptionally clear water — shown as recorded, not corrected.

Sources and method

Enterococcus from the Citizens' Water Quality Testing programme (SwimmableNYC / Billion Oyster Project), plus off-season sampling processed by the New York Harbor School. Dissolved oxygen, temperature and salinity from the Gowanus Dredgers' own field sonde. Turbidity from the Gowanus Remediation Team's weekly Water Quality Monitoring reports, published at gowanussuperfund.com.

The 110 MPN/100 mL line is the US EPA Beach Action Value for Enterococcus in a single sample — the level at which a beach would be posted as unsafe for swimming. The 5 mg/L dissolved-oxygen line is the New York State chronic standard for these waters. Only aggregate figures are published here.
